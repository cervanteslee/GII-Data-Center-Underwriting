## Executive summary

This independent GII research note introduces a commercial real estate approach to underwriting AI data center infrastructure in power-constrained Silicon Valley. A **hypothetical 10 MW critical-IT-capacity facility** links site selection, entitlement, utility readiness, development scope, construction financing, lease acceptance, operating costs, tenant credit, and terminal value into one investment case.

The paper separates the **property landlord's rent and real estate cash flows** from the **neocloud tenant's computing revenue and GPU capital requirements**. It examines public disclosures and financing considerations involving CoreWeave, Nebius, and GMI Cloud as credit-analysis context, not as actual parties to the modeled property transaction.

### Illustrative 10 MW underwriting case

| Measure | Base-case result |
| --- | ---: |
| Critical IT capacity | 10 MW |
| Total Development Cost (excluding tenant GPUs) | US$207.73 million |
| Developer equity | US$90.79 million |
| Contractual capacity rent | US$225 / IT kW / month |
| Annual capacity rent at full billability | US$27.00 million |
| Year-one property net operating income | US$19.75 million |
| Development Yield on total cost | 9.51% |
| Annualized levered equity IRR, including assumed sale | 16.81% |

*All amounts above are illustrative model outputs, not observed project economics, financing offers, market quotations, or forecasts of actual tenant performance. The assumed sale materially affects total modeled returns.*

## Core underwriting questions

1. **Power and delivery:** Is the required IT capacity contractually deliverable at this site, cost, and date?
2. **Development and capital:** Who pays for land, utility work, permanent facility systems, commissioning, and delays?
3. **Lease and cash conversion:** What triggers billability, who is the legal obligor, and what credit support can actually be enforced?
4. **Investment resilience:** How do rent, energy burden, financing, delivery delays, refinancing, and terminal valuation change investor outcomes?

## Methodology and limitations

The development property, site dimensions, tenant, rent, utility availability, construction schedule, financing, and exit assumptions are **hypothetical teaching-case inputs**. No parcel, power commitment, construction bid, executed lease, tenant guarantee, or lender commitment is established for the modeled project. Company and market evidence is dated; the research information cutoff is **October 22, 2026**.

The workbook is the numerical system of record. The companion PDF presents print-ready snapshots and **does not contain editable formulas**. The 72-page paper includes the 17 investor exhibit tables in its appendix; its narrative and PDF are not automatically updated when Excel inputs change. Independent technical, legal, financial, and site-specific diligence remains necessary.
